## What it is
A short announcement post by Andrew Gelman (posted 10 November 2020) on his widely read statistics blog, introducing the 77-page "Bayesian Workflow" paper. It names the co-authors — Aki Vehtari, Daniel Simpson, Charles C. Margossian, Bob Carpenter, Yuling Yao, Paul-Christian Bürkner, Lauren Kennedy, Jonah Gabry, Martin Modrák, and Gelman — quotes the paper's abstract, and explains the motivation for codifying workflow as methodology. The blog is a prominent venue in applied Bayesian statistics, but the post itself is informal commentary, not peer-reviewed.

## Key content for VeriBayes
- Quotes the paper's abstract: workflow spans iterative model building, model checking, validation and troubleshooting of computational problems, model understanding, and model comparison — not just inference.
- Confirms the full author list and the 2020 date of the foundational workflow paper.
- Frames the agenda: in practice many models are fit per problem, and only a subset informs conclusions — workflow makes that multiplicity explicit.
- States three benefits of formalizing workflow: making good practice explicit opens it to development; a step toward automation; computational tools that handle many models.
- Contains no numeric thresholds, checklists, or diagnostics of its own.
